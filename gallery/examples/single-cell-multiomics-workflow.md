# Single-Cell Multiomics Workflow

## Use Case

Methods workflow for a grant, paper, or poster showing tissue collection, single-cell profiling, multiomics integration, and biological interpretation.

## Layout Plan

- Canvas: landscape 16:9
- Reading direction: left to right
- Zones: sample, wet lab, sequencing, computational analysis, biological insight
- Main visual hierarchy: workflow clarity over decorative biology

## Ready-to-Use Prompt

```text
Create a publication-ready biomedical schematic, BioRender-inspired but fully original.

Topic: Single-cell multiomics workflow from patient tissue to integrated cell-state atlas.
Figure type: experimental workflow.
Audience/context: methods section, grant proposal, or conference poster.
Canvas: landscape 16:9, clean white background.

Layout:
Create a five-step left-to-right workflow with compact icons connected by arrows: patient tissue sample, single-cell dissociation, scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq profiling, computational integration, cell-state atlas and pathway interpretation.

Main visual elements:
- tissue biopsy
- dissociated single cells
- microfluidic droplet or plate
- RNA reads
- chromatin accessibility peaks
- computer analysis screen
- UMAP-like cell clusters
- pathway enrichment output
- annotated immune, stromal, and epithelial cell states

Mechanism flow:
1. Tissue biopsy is collected from patient samples.
2. Tissue is dissociated into viable single cells.
3. Single cells undergo scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq profiling.
4. Data are integrated computationally into a shared latent space.
5. Cell states are annotated and linked to pathway activity.
6. Results support biological interpretation and target discovery.

Labels to include exactly:
Tissue biopsy, Single cells, scRNA-seq, scATAC-seq, Integration, Cell-state atlas, Pathways.

Visual style:
Clean vector-like biomedical workflow, restrained multi-hue palette, crisp icons, consistent arrow style, minimal text, white background, subtle shadows, professional methods-figure design, clear spacing between steps.

Avoid:
photorealistic lab photos, cluttered dashboards, copied icons, logos, watermark, fake tiny text, excessive charts, decorative gradients, dark background.
```
